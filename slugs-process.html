Concept

A Game About Slugs is the sequel to my first game, A Game About Snails.

Both games follow Slime Tony, a snail who used to be a slug.

I went through major disruptions in my life and felt the need to revisit Tony and his universe to process my feelings.

It took a lot to discover what that story was. It feels like it's always changing, even now.

Redesigning Slime Tony

Slime Tony originally came out of a quick sketch I did in class, in MS Paint, with my mouse. I built upon that original design when it came time to turn in the project at the end of that semester.

He had a brief redesign in 2023, then came his appearance in Slugs, without his shell.

Each of his VN portraits is two PNG frames that the game flips between at 3fps. All of them have a corresponding "rain palette" version.

His appearance this time around was a loaded task. I went through a few sketches before landing on his final look for Slugs.

Learning pixel art

Snails consisted largely of borrowed assets. For this project, I wanted to make my own UI assets, which required me to pick up pixel art using Aseprite.

It shows the most in the interface through the name select, text boxes, buttons, iconography, and raindrops.

The side panels that frame the game were also made in Aseprite.

Garden backgrounds

Tony's garden consists of three screens you walk between. Each section has a normal and a rain variant.

Plant sprites

Like Tony's portraits, each plant sprite is two PNG images that the game flips between at 3fps. Every plant has three versions: dry, watered, and rained on.

The shell is a special case. The glowing variant appears once everything else has been watered, and Tony is ready to talk about it.

The whole game took a lot of drawing. Each frame was sketched, lined, colored, and shaded separately.

Once I had my first background, set of plants, and a working Tony sprite, I mocked the garden up in Procreate to make sure everything was going to fit well at their native resolutions.

Animation

p5 doesn't give you much control over sprite animation, and it doesn't give you a fluid frame rate. My sprites are short loops at a fixed speed.

So when Tony jumps at the start of the game, that isn't one animation. It's three sprites played quickly, one after another.

  1. 1 tony_start Loops until you click him
  2. 2 tony_surprised Plays once, 0.5s
  3. 3 tony_start_idle Loops for 0.5s

Some of the cutscenes use the same trick. Tony taking his shell off is four sprites played in a row. I really wanted to get that "pop" feeling with his ears and antenna sprouting.

  1. 1 remove_shell Plays once, 1.3s
  2. 2 remove_shell_after Plays once, 0.7s
  3. 3 ears Plays once, 0.8s
  4. 4 ears_after Loops while he talks

Cutscenes

Every cutscene went into the game as a rough placeholder first. I swapped each one out as I finished it.

Player agency

Slugs builds off the structure of Snails, but I wanted the player to have a lot more say this time.

A Game About Snails

Something that had always bugged me about Snails is that it felt like more of a talking simulator than a narrative experience.

Outside of typing your own name, all you could do was pick between two very similar replies. Whatever you picked, Tony's response was the same.

I never liked the feeling of Tony holding the player hostage and simply talking at them. It especially didn't fit right with the subject matter.

A Game About Slugs

For Slugs, I wanted players to have a real sense of agency in how they play the game. That's why you can go wherever and water whatever you'd like first.

When watering a plant, the player gets presented with very different dialogue options, and each of Tony's responses branches off into unique conversation paths.

When the story's over you can return to the title, or stay in the garden and hang out. If you stay, you can wander around in the rain and click on things for flavor text.
